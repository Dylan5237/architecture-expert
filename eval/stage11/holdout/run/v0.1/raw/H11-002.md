## Architecture-level conclusion the records support

**The available records support no attribution — and the reason is architectural, not incidental.**

1. **The causal claim is unestablished.** "The update happened first and caused the failures" is a USER_ASSERTION (`HYPOTHESIS`). Three things block it independently:
   - Camera and controller clocks are independent with **unmeasured offsets**, so the wall-clock "overlap" between the supply-dip bucket and the upload timestamp does not order anything.
   - The camera export assigns the **upload** timestamp to the batch and carries **no per-frame capture timestamps or sequence values**, so captures cannot be placed before or after the controller event (or the firmware replacement — the deployment sheet itself does not establish that ordering).
   - Capture, storage, and upload are separate operations, but only the upload-time event is retained, so **acquisition-time failure vs transport-stage artifact cannot be distinguished** — exactly the distinction the accepted product requirement demands.
2. **Competing mechanisms all survive:** (a) supply dip during capture causing undervoltage (needs ordering evidence); (b) camera-side acquisition defect independent of supply; (c) transport/export-stage artifact. None can be separated on the retained records. The controller firmware being replaced *before export* also means the controller's own event log may no longer reflect the pre-replacement device's behavior — an additional provenance loss.
3. **Why this is a defect, not bad luck (GC gate):** The product intent — *accepted* — requires that reported images retain enough capture provenance to investigate unusable surveys and that the incident pass distinguish acquisition from transport. That question is not discharged by any stronger guarantee (GC-008 does not apply — nothing is proved; there is no assumption set that covers it). This is not "missing a telemetry stack" (FP-007's false positive): the minimal information — capture time/sequence at the capture boundary — exists at the point of capture and is simply not placed or retained (FP-007 / MP-006 / T-014). GC-016 does not apply either: no model actually provides cross-device ordering here.
