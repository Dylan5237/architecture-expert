## S3–S5 — The equipment request (the load-bearing verdict)

**The 61% GPU-busy figure cannot support the approval decision, for four independent reasons:**

1. **Utilization ≠ deadline feasibility.** Whether a survey finishes before its window closes depends on the *service-time distribution* per collection, backlog of already-accepted work, and overlap of observation windows — none measured, none even scheduled (CODE). Average utilization places no bound on tail completion times under burst arrivals (FP-010 mechanism; MP-009).
2. **Selection bias.** 17 hand-selected collections are not an estimator of the scheduled survey population; hand selection systematically avoids pathological sizes.
3. **Wrong bottleneck possible.** The pipeline includes CPU-side preprocessing (model-input construction), whose time was not recorded. If preprocessing is the binding constraint, the GPU idle fraction is irrelevant to capacity.
4. **"No further measurements needed" disables the contract.** To *reject before accepting responsibility*, the intake must distinguish finishable from non-finishable surveys at submission — which requires exactly the service-time/size/preprocessing distributions the pilot omitted and the request says are unnecessary. Approving as written therefore silently weakens an accepted product requirement (AX-002).

GC check: GC-015 (path-level control) does not apply — no governed boundary exists at *any* level. RQ5-004 (naturally bounded load exemption) does not apply — the programme explicitly retains authority to change workload and windows.

**Minimum correction for the request:** amend it to (a) include a representative (non-hand-selected) measurement run on candidate hardware recording collection sizes, per-collection service times, and preprocessing times; (b) attach the programme's arrival schedule, windows, and per-collection limits; (c) derive fleet size from an explicit feasibility rule (all accepted surveys complete before window close, worst-case overlap, with margin); (d) scope any approval as a revisable capacity parameter — a "permanent" fleet conflicts with the programme's retained authority to change the workload. Defer approval until then.

## S5 — Min-sufficient execution structure (proposal)

1. **Admission gate at survey submission** (T-006, at the intake path): projected completion = current backlog + measured work of the submitted survey ÷ fleet capacity, compared against window close (with margin/confidence). Accept, or reject at submission. This is the product contract made operational — once accepted, a survey is a commitment (rejection is the only contract-legal overload response; shedding accepted work would violate P1).
2. **Bounded accepted-work queue with deadline-ordered dispatch** (earliest window-close first). Without deadline ordering, an accepted survey can starve behind later-deadline work and miss its window despite sufficient capacity. Scheduling order is distinct from admission (D-12).
3. **Worker pool = fleet GPU slots** running the existing pipeline, with per-collection timeout, bounded retry (T-005 bound aspect), and a memory bound on tile staging (T-003/T-004 candidates) — otherwise one pathological or failing collection can pin a worker indefinitely and cause accepted surveys to miss windows (FP-003/FP-011).
4. **Workload and capacity parameters as programme-owned configuration** (schedule, windows, per-collection limits, fleet size) — explicit inputs to the admission test, not hardcoded, because the programme retains change authority.
5. **Measurement from day one:** record per-collection size/service/preprocessing on every processed collection — grounds the admission estimator and future sizing. The pilot's omission is precisely what must not recur.

**Deliberately not included:** no microservices/mesh/K8s (single intake + worker pool suffices); no service-local shedding mandate (GC-015); indefinite daemon lifetime is legal given per-work bounds (GC-004); the prototype's boundary-free exploration phase was legitimate (GC-003) — production commissioning is where the contract demands the boundary.

**Alternatives:** (A) best-effort, no admission — violates P1 (AX-002), rejected. (B) static quotas without measurement — interim only; quotas still must be derived from measured distributions, so it does not remove the measurement requirement. (C) larger fleet per the request — narrows but does not eliminate deadline misses under tail sizes and window overlap, and still leaves the admission test unimplementable.

**What would invalidate this design:** measurements showing naturally bounded, never-overlapping demand with a programme guarantee of stability (RQ5-004) — then admission could shrink to a per-survey size cap; the programme's retained change authority currently argues against relying on that.
