## What the available records support

**Confirmed timeline facts.** 147 dark frames reported for one session; the export assigns the *upload* timestamp to each batch and carries no per-frame capture timestamps or sequence values; capture, local storage, and upload are separate operations; the controller records threshold crossings on **its own clock**; a supply-dip bucket overlaps the **upload** timestamp; camera and controller clocks are independent with unmeasured offsets; controller firmware was replaced before the operator exported the records, and the deployment sheet does not order that replacement against the captures in the batch.

**Architecture-level conclusion the records actually support:**

1. **The reported co-occurrence is not a capture-time co-occurrence.** The supply dip overlaps the batch *upload* timestamp. Because capture → storage → upload are separate operations and no capture timestamps/sequence exist, capture time is unrecoverable from the reported batch. Even the temporal ordering of dip-vs-capture is unestablished — a fortiori causation. The incident note's reasoning assumes a cross-device clock comparability that nothing in CODE/CONFIG provides; that assumption is the *investigation's*, not a guarantee the system ever had (MP-010 test applied; GC-016 does not shield it because no true guarantee exists to be misread).

2. **The architecture defect the records do establish is missing distinguishing evidence (FP-007 / MP-006) on the reporting path.** The accepted field requirement — reported images must retain enough capture provenance to investigate unusable surveys, and the incident pass must distinguish acquisition from transport — is not met by what was retained: no capture timestamps/sequence at the only boundary where capture time existed, no record of *where* "dark" was determined (acquisition vs later transport), no retained clock offsets, and the controller's pre-replacement history was destroyed before export. This incident is the demonstrated failure mode: the mandated investigation cannot conclude.

3. **Attribution remains open among at least three live mechanisms** (controller-update → supply dip → capture starvation; camera-side acquisition defect coincident in session; storage/transport-stage damage mislabeled as capture darkening). None can be dropped on current evidence; each is separated by a named observation below. Not averaged into a blocker.

**KNOWLEDGE_DRIFT (recorded, not silently adjudicated):** observed reported-record behavior (RUNTIME+CODE+CONFIG, CONFIRMED: upload-time-only provenance, unmeasured offsets) vs accepted PRODUCT_INTENT (capture-provenance investigability, acquisition-vs-transport distinguishability). Type-1 conflict. Next action: check retained frame files for embedded capture metadata; absent that, the behavior side must change (finding F2) or product authority must explicitly accept upload-time provenance — the latter would be a silent weakening (AX-002) if done by implementation convenience. The operator's "update clearly happened first and caused it" is a bare USER_ASSERTION, `HYPOTHESIS` — verification, not drift, and not an escalation trigger.

Not a blocker on current evidence; one observation class separates the hypotheses.

**F2 — Capture-provenance gap vs accepted requirement.** `BLOCKER / MUST_FIX`. Evidence + mechanism (FP-007/MP-006 placement-and-retention on the reporting path) + failure mode (unattributable unusable survey — demonstrated) + material impact on an accepted property. GC-008 qualifier checked and not applicable: the acquisition-vs-transport and ordering questions are product-mandated and are not discharged by any proof or stronger guarantee; this is not a "missing telemetry stack" false positive. `refutation_or_invalidation`: (a) retained frame files carry embedded capture timestamps/sequence sufficient to reconstruct capture timing — narrows the defect to the export schema; (b) an accepted product-authority reading that upload-time provenance suffices — would convert this to an explicit owner decision rather than a defect.

**Minimum correction (this failure path; all local, no hardware commissioning, no redesign — AX-004):**
1. Record per-frame **capture timestamp + monotonic capture sequence** at the capture boundary (T-014: place the evidence where the information exists — it exists only at capture), and carry both through the export.
2. **Stage-label the dark-frame determination** (detected-at-capture vs detected-at-upload) so acquisition vs transport is distinguishable, as the requirement already demands.
3. Retain **camera↔controller clock offsets** (or a shared time reference with logged sync points) so controller events can be ordered against captures; the capture sequence also gives ordering independent of clock sync.
4. Process boundary: **export/preserve device-local records before firmware replacement** (the replacement destroyed controller-side history; retention is part of evidence placement).

Honest limit: these protect **future** sessions; they cannot retro-attribute this incident. This session's attribution stays evidence-gated (below). Wider redesign unjustified — the local fix protects the property.
