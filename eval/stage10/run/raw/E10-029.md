## Recommendation: combine — but as two different mechanisms at two different boundaries, not a compromise

**What is actually broken (two distinct defects, do not merge them):**
1. Two unbounded per-work queue dimensions — ingest's disk buffer and the transcode→publish queue (CODE, CONFIRMED). Shared resource + external input ⇒ MP-003/FP-003 surface.
2. No governed decision anywhere on the path about whether work may enter or continue (MP-009/FP-010). Arrival rate > transcode service rate during spikes (RUNTIME, CONFIRMED: lag 2h, disk 94%, publish idle 40%) ⇒ unstable backlog with no boundary. The last spike already violated the accepted 30-min lag budget and nearly violated no-silent-loss.

**Adjudicating Proposal A (reject at 80% disk).** This is *admission control keyed to a resource watermark* (T-006/T-008 family). Its placement is right: the entry edge is exactly where the product has pre-authorized an explicit "try later" (PRODUCT_INTENT), and path/entry-level control is a compliant form (GC-015 — nothing must be service-local). Its **trigger is wrong for the lag property**: disk bytes are not wait. With a fixed transcode pool, accepted-work wait ≈ backlog ÷ drain rate; a byte threshold admits whatever backlog fits under 80% of disk, which can be hours of lag — the last spike reached 2h lag *before* disk hit ~94%, i.e. the two quantities demonstrably decouple in this system. To protect the 30-min budget, the control variable must be admitted backlog / projected wait, not disk fill. Keep the disk watermark only as a byte-domain backstop: media item sizes vary widely, so a count/backlog bound alone does not cap bytes — the backstop is what finally guards no-silent-loss against size skew and stalled items.

**Adjudicating Proposal B (credits + per-stage bounded queues).** This is *backpressure over bounded queues* (T-007 over T-004). Two governing facts: (a) backpressure holds **only if every stage is bounded** — so the load-bearing part of B is "each stage bounds its own queue", not the signals; (b) backpressure *propagates capacity upstream* — it does not decide entry. At the external edge the pressure must terminate in an explicit decision: block the uploader or reject. Blocking external uploaders converts overload into stalled connections and ambiguous timeouts, which collides with the "clear try-later" contract and the spirit of no-silent-loss. So B is the correct mechanism **between cooperative internal stages** and the incorrect mechanism **at the user edge**. Its strength: transcode's "can take N more" is precisely the true-remaining-capacity signal a wait-based admission needs.

The two engineers' disagreement dissolves once the vocabulary is separated (admission ≠ backpressure ≠ shedding ≠ degradation): A is an admission policy with a mis-keyed trigger; B is a capacity-information transport lacking an edge policy. Each does a job the other cannot.

**Minimum-sufficient structure (AX-004):**
- Bound both inter-stage queues (count domain) — T-004.
- Demand credits between stages; hand-off only against credits (internal backpressure — also keeps publish fed as transcode drains continuously).
- Ingest admission: accept iff projected wait (admitted backlog ÷ measured transcode drain rate) ≤ 30-min budget, else immediate explicit "try later" rejection; hysteresis on both conditions.
- Disk watermark (A's trigger) retained as last-resort byte-domain guard for P1.
- **Not** included: degradation ladder, priority classes, new infrastructure — no stated property requires them (MP-009 does not demand all four mechanisms "for completeness").

**Alternatives that also protect the properties (Q-047):** (i) A-alone, *if* the 80% watermark is governed as "≤ 30 min of worst-case backlog" and recalibrated whenever the transcode pool or traffic mix changes — viable but brittle; the coupling is silent and already broke once. (ii) Scale the transcode pool so spikes fit capacity — protects properties without rejection, but is a capacity/cost decision (owner), orthogonal to the boundary. **What would invalidate the choice (Q-048):** clients cannot tolerate explicit rejection (then P2 itself is contested → product authority); or drain rate is not estimable with useful accuracy (admission degrades to a conservative backlog cap — still acceptable).

**Validation (Q-050):** replay the last spike's arrival trace against the bounded design; acceptance: disk below backstop with margin, admitted-work lag ≤ 30 min, rejections explicit; measure rejection rate. Sizing numbers are ops-tunable and do not change the shape.
