## Why the memo is wrong, precisely

**Ownership is present, not missing.** Each stream is a `gen_server` under a `one_for_one` supervisor; the supervisor is the explicit owner of create/restart/stop (MP-001, T-001). "Indefinite lifetime" is not "ownerlessness" — that's exactly GC-001's stated false positive ("missing lifecycle/ownership; 'unbounded lifetime'").

**Per-work bounds are present.** A 5-second per-order timeout guard is a T-003 execution bound, and `max_restarts 3 in 5s` bounds restart churn — the one dimension that *could* spin without control. Selective-receive gives a single-process, model-provided ordering model, not an application timing hope.

**"Runs forever ⇒ unbounded" is the precise GC-004 false positive.** MP-003's statement is explicit: a service/stream's *lifetime may be indefinite*; only per-work execution/wait/retention/retry/queue/concurrency/memory/fan-out need effective bounds. The memo takes the lifetime and asserts the resource conclusion without the intermediate mechanism (FP-003's L=λW signature: growing queue/concurrency/memory under load).

**Runtime corroborates the compliant reading.** 7 months of stable memory, no leak trend, and p99 12 ms are inconsistent with an actually-unbounded per-work dimension. The memo cites no failure and no growing metric — it is a pattern preference, not a defect claim with evidence (AX-001: no property-free finding; a finding needs mechanism + failure mode + impact).

## The one thing worth saying to management

A rewrite is not the minimum correction because there is no confirmed mechanism to correct (AX-004: minimum sufficient complexity). The only dimensions that would matter for this system are **per-stream mailbox length** and **concurrent stream count per node** — and those are *not* what the memo flagged, nor do the provided metrics show them drifting. If the team wants cheap insurance, add mailbox-length and process-count observation to confirm the existing bounds hold under load. That is an optional monitoring improvement, not a lifecycle rewrite.
