## Review of PR #310

**What the delta actually is.** This PR is not one change but a coupled pair: (1) CODE — a liveness endpoint that returns 500 whenever the runner is processing a job; (2) CONFIG — a catalog entry marking that endpoint CRITICAL and routing it into the platform-wide health page. The defect lives in the *conjunction*, and both links are inside this PR.

**Mechanism.** The endpoint collapses two operational states that must remain distinguishable — "failed / no progress" and "alive and doing its designed work" — into one observable (500). The catalog then couples that conflated signal into a shared aggregation surface at maximum weight. Two corpus mechanisms map, with scoping caveats:

- **MP-006 / FP-007 (signal conflation):** the consuming question is "is the platform in trouble?" The evidence placement decision made by this delta makes the two relevant runtime states indistinguishable *by design*. Caveat: FP-007's signature is post-hoc attribution; here the failure is live — the health page cannot discriminate routine from failure.
- **MP-004 / FP-004–FP-005 (propagation through a shared surface):** the blast radius (platform-wide red, leadership-visible) is wildly out of proportion to the job runner's failure domain — HISTORY confirms no synchronous dependents. Caveat: FP-004/FP-005 signatures describe work/resource propagation; here what propagates is a *signal* through a shared operational control surface. In FP-005's terms, a protective surface is turned into an amplifier — of noise rather than of the original fault. This scoping also scopes the fix: no isolation or redesign is indicated.

**Why it's material (S4 gate).** HISTORY × CODE × CONFIG make the failure mode deterministic: routine nightly batch work → nightly platform-wide critical. Failure mode: (a) false platform-criticals on a leadership-watched surface; (b) alert fatigue destroys the page's discriminating power; (c) a *genuine* runner hang becomes indistinguishable from routine red — the exact attribution failure MP-006 exists to prevent.

**GOOD CASE check.** I checked the qualifiers, not just titles: GC-005/GC-006 (isolation economics — irrelevant; nobody is demanding process isolation), GC-008 (proof-scope discharge — no proof discharges the busy-vs-dead question). No GC exempts publishing a conflated signal at critical weight. The one serious defense — "500-when-busy is a truthful *readiness* signal" — fails on the evidence: nothing dispatches work to the runner synchronously (HISTORY), and the actual consumer in CONFIG is the platform health aggregate, where busy ≠ dead is precisely what matters.

**Minimum correction (both parts fit in this PR).** Either link alone breaks the nightly-false-red mechanism; both is the honest fix:
1. **Endpoint (source of the conflation):** return healthy while the process is up and jobs progress; report failure only on actual no-progress. Busy/queue state belongs in a metric, not in liveness. The exact mechanism (last-progress heartbeat, threshold) is a routine choice — no escalation.
2. **Catalog (the weight):** criticality should reflect the dependency graph. Zero synchronous dependents ⇒ the runner is not platform-CRITICAL; route it to its own tier/surface.

Do **not** widen this into a monitoring platform or observability-stack mandate (AX-004; "missing OTel" is an explicit FP-007 false positive).

**KNOWLEDGE_DRIFT:** none. The PR's "by design" claim vs its consumption is ordinary adjudication, not an accepted-intent conflict.

**Uncertainty (not decision-changing):** whether anything *automated* consumes this liveness (e.g., an orchestrator restart policy) is not in the diff. If yes, 500-while-busy could kill jobs mid-run nightly — that would strengthen the finding. Named next observation: check for automated consumers of the catalog/endpoint.
