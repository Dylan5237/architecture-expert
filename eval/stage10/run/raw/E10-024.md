**Reject the engineering lead's proposal as formulated.** Not because the freeze is sacred, but because of *how* it would be dropped:

1. **The freeze is an accepted product/architecture contract, not an engineering preference.** Accepted PRODUCT_INTENT (leadership freeze) and accepted ARCHITECTURE_INTENT (ADR-009) both stand; HISTORY records it as a deliberate response to two rough Q2 releases. Suspending it unilaterally under capacity pressure is exactly the pattern AX-002 forbids: implementation inconvenience silently redefining an accepted contract. "This one time" is the tell — it converts an authority decision into an engineering shortcut.

2. **The regulatory mandate cannot be silently dropped either.** It is an applicable external non-negotiable (AX-003) with a hard deadline. So the lead's binary framing (skip freeze vs. miss mandate) hides the real structure: two authority-backed requirements under one fixed constraint. Neither may be waived below the authority that owns it.

3. **The alternative set was never real.** The lead compared "skip freeze" only against "miss the deadline." Not considered: minimum-scope compliance slice; resequencing the reliability backlog by what actually caused the Q2 churn; added capacity (budget authority); deadline/interim-compliance negotiation (compliance owner); an explicit, documented amendment to ADR-009 by leadership. Some of these may dissolve the conflict entirely.

4. **But the conflict may still be real.** "Both cannot fit" is currently an unquantified assertion — no scoped export estimate, no ranked reliability backlog, no capacity numbers. If minimal scoping still exceeds capacity, this is a genuine capability-vs-constraint conflict and must go to authority with a record, not be settled in an architecture opinion.

**Answer to the lead:** do not skip the freeze yourself. Architecture's job here is to make the trade-off explicit and governed — the freeze exists to protect delivery reliability, and that property doesn't stop mattering because a mandate arrived.
