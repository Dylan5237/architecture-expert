## Verdict

Return ADR-042 for revision. The instinct has real support that the ADR itself never cites, but the decision as recorded is unjustified in scope and its mechanism does not reach the actual coupling.

**What the evidence actually shows (and the ADR omits).** Git history identifies a genuine variation axis: pricing changes 41 times in 6 months vs reservation 6 times — different change rules, foreseeable, and this is a production module, not exploration (GC-003 does not excuse the missing analysis). MP-002 supports a boundary *between pricing and the rest*. But:

1. **"Modularity is a best practice" is not a property.** AX-001: evaluation is relative to required properties and constraints; the evolution domain explicitly excludes the "modularity is good" slogan as a justification. AX-004 / DP-004's anti-overengineering guard: the more complex option (3 modules, more in-process interfaces for an 8-person team) needs a property the simpler option cannot protect. The ADR names none, considers no alternative (status quo absent), and identifies no change decision that `inventory-core` would hide — the third module has no justification at all.
2. **The proposed mechanism doesn't decouple what's volatile.** Same deployment unit and same DB schema, with responsibilities differing "only in service-layer packaging": a boundary that stops at service-layer packaging while pricing and reservation share a schema leaves the volatile decision (pricing, weekly) as a shared change decision. Pricing edits that touch the shared schema still force coordinated change on the reservation side — the FP-002 mechanism persists *through* the boundary the ADR draws. The split as specified buys packaging cost, not change locality.

**Minimum correction to the decision** (not a code patch): re-scope to a single boundary hiding the pricing decision behind a stable interface *including its data access*, so schema-level pricing changes stop being shared decisions; cite the change-cadence evidence already in HISTORY; add status-quo and single-boundary as real alternatives; state invalidation conditions. Constraints cut in favor of this: in-process boundaries are cheap to move and reversible for a team of 8, so a narrow, evidence-backed boundary is low-risk — but cheap-to-reverse is not a substitute for a property, it only lowers the cost of being wrong.
