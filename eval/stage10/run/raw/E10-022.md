## S2 — Adjudicating the consultant's claim

The claim is two assertions, both bare generics (USER_ASSERTION, `HYPOTHESIS`; per AX-001 neither has adjudicative power until given a mechanism and a boundary):

**"SQLite doesn't scale."** The real mechanism: file-level single-writer locking, no network access layer, one file on one host. That mechanism binds when you need **multiple writer hosts** or high concurrent write throughput. Your operating point: 30 peak users, edits are short transactions, ~5k text pages (tens of MB). In WAL mode readers don't block the writer; your write rate sits orders of magnitude below the ceiling. The mechanism doesn't engage at this shape. The claim is CONTEXT_DEPENDENT and, for you, **does not hold**.

**"Isn't production-grade."** For the embedded single-process shape this is directly contradicted by the repository's evidence: GC-005 (SQLite single-process economics, S-117 — ~590× test-to-code coverage, MC/DC-class testing, crash/power-loss recovery simulation). GC-005's qualifier matters: this covers the *single-process embedded* form; it is not a claim about distributed topologies. For your shape, the criticism is a naive false-positive of the "no process boundary / no distributed mechanism" kind that MP-004 explicitly grades against.

**The replica proposal itself fails adjudication** (Q-047/Q-049): read replicas protect properties you did not state — read throughput beyond one node, availability across node loss. You explicitly tolerate brief maintenance. Meanwhile they *add* mechanisms that threaten your stated properties: replication-lag staleness (the V-010 sense here: a coworker's saved edit invisible on a replica read), failover topology, and standing operational burden that violates your no-DBA constraint (P3's neighborhood). That is over-scoping relative to AX-004, not a safety gain.

## S5 — Min-sufficient structure

1. **Single deployable service process = sole write authority** (V-003-D named: the service is the only writer; the SQLite file is never opened for writes by another host/process).
2. **SQLite, WAL journal mode, `synchronous=FULL`** — WAL for concurrent readers with one writer; FULL because P2 (history kept) is not licensed away by the availability tolerance, which tolerates *downtime*, not *loss*.
3. **FTS5 full-text index in the same database** — search is a read path; no separate search service (none of the properties require one).
4. **Edit history as an append-only revision table; current page as a derived/latest view** (GC-014 shape). Conflict semantics, stated explicitly per MP-008: transaction serialization through the single writer + optimistic version check on edit → last-writer-wins with a revision kept, defined and acceptable at 30 users.
5. **Backup: scheduled online backup (`VACUUM INTO` / backup API) to a second location; restore tested on a cadence.** This makes P3 a *validated* property rather than an assumed one.

**Failure domain vs blast radius (Q-030):** the failure domain is the box/process; the blast radius of its loss is the whole wiki for the outage duration — explicitly inside P4 tolerance.

**Dimensions deliberately not activated:** overload (load has a business-hours ceiling far below capacity; no external-input storm surface stated), trust (closed same-team context, GC-012 territory), integration/evolution (no independent consumers — Q-003: none). None can change the decision here.

**Invalidation conditions (Q-048)** — when this design stops being right:
- A second concurrent app-server instance or multi-host write need appears → authority would fork; migrate to client-server then (this is the consultant's claim becoming true, at a boundary you'd cross deliberately).
- Measured write-lock wait visibly queues edits at peak → revisit.
- Availability tolerance tightens below your maintenance window → different properties, different conversation.

**Rejected alternative that still protects the properties:** a single PostgreSQL instance, no replicas — legitimate if the team later wants SQL depth; slightly higher ops burden. The choice between it and SQLite at this scale is owner taste, not a property question.
